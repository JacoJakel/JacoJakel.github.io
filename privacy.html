CalorieTrack privacy policy

Last updated: 30 September 2026

CalorieTrack is an app to keep track of your food, pantry, training, steps and sleep. This policy explains what data it handles, where it is stored and who it is shared with. In short: your data stays on your phone; the app has no servers of its own and no user accounts.

Who is responsible

CalorieTrack is made by an independent developer. For any privacy question: jacobolssondesign@gmail.com.

Data stored on your phone

  • Your profile: name, sex, year of birth, height, weight and goals.
  • Your food diary, recipes and own dishes, pantry and shopping list.
  • Your training, steps, walks (with their GPS route) and sleep and heart-rate records.
  • The photos you take of your meals and your shopping.

All of this stays in the app's private storage on your device. It is not uploaded to any server of the developer. Uninstalling the app deletes it.

Permissions and what they are for

  • Camera: to photograph meals and shopping.
  • Physical activity: to count your steps.
  • Location: only while you record a walk, to draw its route. It is not used in the background outside a running walk.
  • Bluetooth: to connect a heart-rate strap or a ring and read your heart rate and sleep.
  • Notifications and alarms: for your meal, training, walk, expiry and sleep reminders.

Data that leaves your phone

  • Artificial intelligence (Google Gemini): when you ask the app to read a photo (a meal or your shopping), that photo and any text you add are sent to the Google Gemini API to identify the foods. Short texts are also sent to describe an exercise or the steps of a recipe. Google processes this data under its Gemini API terms. The developer does not keep those photos.
  • Reports: if you use “Report a problem”, the reason, your message and the context (the recipe or the day) are sent to the developer's email through the Web3Forms service. Only when you send it.
  • Ads (Google AdMob): the app shows Google ads. To do so, Google may collect the device's advertising ID, technical device data, the IP address and an approximate location derived from it, and use them to serve and measure ads. In the European Economic Area and the UK you are asked for consent before ads are shown, and you can change it at any time in Profile › Privacy. If you don't give it, the app still shows ads, but not personalised ones. More in how Google uses advertising data.
  • Maps: when you view a walk's route, the app downloads the map images (OpenStreetMap and, in satellite view, ArcGIS). Those servers receive your IP address and the map area shown.
  • Online shopping: if you use “Shop online”, the Amazon store opens inside the app; what you do there is covered by Amazon's privacy policy.

The developer does not sell your data or use it for anything other than what is described here.

Health

CalorieTrack gives nutrition, energy and rest estimates for information only. It is not a medical device and does not replace the advice of a health professional.

Children

The app is intended for adults and is not directed at children under 16.

Your rights

Because your data is on your phone, you can view, correct and delete it from within the app, or delete all of it by uninstalling. For the data Google processes (AI and ads) you can exercise your rights with Google. For any question about this policy, write to jacobolssondesign@gmail.com.

Changes

If this policy changes, it will be updated here with its new date.
